How does a cell come to have, and keep, the ion gradients and membrane potential it has? These pages build the answer one mechanism at a time, each kept switched on in the next: diffusion, drift, a membrane, one channel, two, the leaks running the cell down, the pump that stops them, and the transporters that set chloride.

Written for graduate and medical students. Everything runs in this page; nothing is sent anywhere.

The ions

Color names the ion; shape carries the charge: circles are cations (a double ring for Ca²⁺), diamonds are anions, and the large gray shapes are impermeant anions trapped inside the cell (A⁻).

The voltage ruler

A tick at each ion's equilibrium potential, a needle at Vm, and an arrow from the needle toward each tick: that ion's driving force, wider when the membrane conducts it better. Every idea from the one-channel chapter on reads off it. This one shows the healthy cell the compartment chapters start from.
