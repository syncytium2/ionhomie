No forces, no membrane, no charge yet: just ions jostled by water. Each one takes a random step, with no idea where the others are. Yet the crowd as a whole flows from where it is dense to where it is sparse. That flow is Fick's law.

The concentration profile

Bars: the particles above, counted in 40 slices. Line: the exact solution of the diffusion equation for this box, c(x, t), with reflecting walls. The particles are the model here; the line is the check.

Spreading: mean squared distance

⟨Δx²⟩ along x, counting every step each ion takes (the walls fold the walk back, so their folds are undone for this count). It grows in a straight line, 2Dt: twice the time, twice the squared distance.

The math

Fick's first law: the flux J (amount per area per time) runs down the concentration gradient.

J = −D · dc/dx

Conservation turns it into the diffusion equation, whose solution for a box with reflecting walls, starting from a step, is a cosine series:

∂c/∂t = D · ∂²c/∂x² c(x,t) = ½ + Σₙ (2/nπ) sin(nπ/2) cos(nπx/L) exp(−(nπ/L)² D t)

Each particle's random walk has a mean squared displacement along x of 2Dt, so the time to wander a distance x is about x²/2D.

Free-solution diffusion coefficients are recalled table values (K⁺ 1.96, Na⁺ 1.33, Cl⁻ 2.03, Ca²⁺ 0.79 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s at 25 °C) and are still to be checked against a cited source.
