The same cell, gradients and all, sitting at 0 mV: its membrane carries no charge yet. Now put in one kind of channel, a pore that passes only one ion. When it opens, that ion runs down its concentration gradient, and each ion that crosses carries its charge with it. The charge builds a voltage that pushes back, until push and gradient balance. That balance is the drift chapter's zero net flux, now across a membrane.

The charge ledger

In this chapter the concentrations are held fixed. The ledger is the reason that is a fair approximation: the gauges would not move.

The math

Each open channel lets its ion through at a rate set by the GHK flux equation. With only one kind of channel, the current is zero at exactly one voltage: that ion's Nernst potential.

C · dVm/dt = −I_ion(Vm) I_ion = 0 ⇔ Vm = E_ion = (RT/zF) · ln([ion]out / [ion]in)

Close the channel and the current is zero at every voltage: nothing carries charge, so the membrane stays charged. A real membrane always has some leak, and leaks are the next chapter.
