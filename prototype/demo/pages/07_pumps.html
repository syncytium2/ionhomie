The fix for rundown is a machine that carries ions back up their gradients, paid for with ATP. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in for each ATP it splits. Running exactly as fast as the leaks run, it holds every concentration still: the pump–leak steady state. Here is the healthy cell again, pump running.

What to notice

  • Block the pump and Vm jumps a few mV at once, then creeps. The jump is the pump's own current, gone: its direct, electrogenic share of Vm. The creep is the gradients running down, as in the last chapter. The pump's real job is the second thing, not the first.
  • Wash the ouabain out and the cell recovers. High Na⁺ inside drives the pump faster than at rest; the gradients rebuild and the cell shrinks back as the extra solute and water leave.
  • Raise bath K⁺ and Vm depolarizes at once, because E_K moves and K⁺ dominates the resting permeability. That is hyperkalemia's danger to excitable tissue.
  • Stop the PMCA and Ca²⁺ climbs toward its own equilibrium, tens of thousands of times higher. It barely touches Vm: there is too little Ca²⁺ permeability at rest. The danger of Ca²⁺ overload is what it does inside the cell, not to the voltage.
Clinical connections

Digoxin and the other cardiac glycosides partly inhibit the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, as ouabain does completely; the pump-capacity slider shows a partial block. Hyperkalemia raises extracellular K⁺; the bath K⁺ slider shows its first effect, the shift in E_K and Vm. Ischemia takes away the ATP for every pump at once: the rundown chapter.

The cell is generic and its parameters illustrative; this shows mechanisms, not doses or time courses.

The model

The rundown model plus two pumps. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase cycles at a rate set by internal Na⁺ (Hill coefficient 3, half-activation 10 mM) and external K⁺ (Hill coefficient 2, half-activation 1.5 mM); each cycle is 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, one net charge out, one ATP. Its maximum rate is not tuned by hand: it is solved so the healthy cell is an exact steady state at Na⁺ 12 mM and K⁺ 140 mM. The PMCA extrudes Ca²⁺ (Hill coefficient 2, half-activation 0.2 µM), treated as electroneutral; it is solved to hold Ca²⁺ at 0.1 µM. There is no Ca²⁺ buffering, so Ca²⁺ changes here far faster than in a real cell, where most of it is bound.
